Good Ending

The Trial

The words hang in the throne room like smoke after a fire — and then, silence.

The Usurper's sword arm lowers. Not quickly. Not with grace. But it lowers.

The court holds its breath. Your companions hold theirs. You stood your ground not with steel, but with something rarer — patience, reason, and a will that refused to break even when the blows came.

The Usurper is taken into custody before the hour is done. His soldiers, seeing their lord bound and led from the throne room, scatter or surrender. The banners of the rightful crown rise again over the capital.

A tribunal is convened within the fortnight. Lords, clerics, and common folk alike bear witness as the charges are read. The Usurper offers no plea of innocence. He had known, perhaps, that this was always how it would end — with words rather than wounds.

Peace is not simple. It never is. But it is real, and it is lasting. The kingdom breathes again.

You chose mercy — and mercy chose the better future.
