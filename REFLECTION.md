# Reflection: Crownless King

## Decisions

The most important decision in this project was to add a "Final Battle" boss fight at the end of Act III with javascript and HTML/CSS. I wanted the journey to feel epic and conclusive with a big boss battle to conclude an action packed short story that fits into the Dark Fantasy aesthetic I've leaned into for this project. 

Leading into my next decision, another very important decision was choosing to lean into Dark Fantasy as the medium for artistic expression/images/inspiration/aesthetics. The page was purposely set to dark mode when first opening, the images selected for the flavor had to be hand selected by me for every 100+ html pages. 

## What Worked

What worked was how functional and user friendly the final product turned out to be. I've shared the project with numerous friends and family and they've found it to be easy, although they did not appreciate the feature of a Game Over when playing through the story. The branching structure for this project also worked well amd I'm satisfied I was able to explore so many branching paths and different endings to enhance my audience's experience. 

## What could I have done differently?

If I had to choose what I would have done differently, it's to make party members more relevant throughout the story. I also could have added a lightweight save system using localStorage, which I hadn't done. If someone leaves the website they'll lose their progress. Thankfully, the story is very short and can be completed in ~4-5 minutes so the feature was not implemented. Secondly I would have liked to add background art or even animations for the story to enhance immersion which I sadly did not implement.

## What I learned 

This project taught me how powerful simple web technologies can be when used intentionally. I learned that HTML/css, and a bit of JavaScript are more than enough to create an engaging interactive experience when the structure and storytelling are strong.

I also learned a lot about narrative design—specifically how branching paths multiply complexity. Every choice affects pacing, tone, and continuity. Writing for a branching structure requires thinking several steps ahead and constantly checking that each path still feels meaningful. There were times where I felt I was moving the story in a direction that was nonsensical. To mitigate this I had to make the story short, hit the story beats and not expand anymore. If I had expanded more on the characters and story I fear I would not have made the deadline. However, I am very proud of the final prototype and final project results because it turned out very polished.

I learned the importance of scoping and restraint. It’s tempting to add more features, more branches, more polish, but every addition has a cost. Choosing what not to build was just as important as choosing what to include.

Finally, I gained confidence in my ability to take a project from concept to deployment. This project brought together skills from across the CMPA program—storytelling, UX thinking, prototyping, and web development—and showed me how they can work together to create something complete and functional.
